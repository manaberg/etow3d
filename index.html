END OF THE WORLDS 3D

A wizard's war against the orcs, and against other wizards. Pick a mode; the full rules of each are below.

Battle

Command regiments of orcs against a rival wizard's army on open fields. Five battles, up to 200 a side.

Survival

Ten floors, seven orc classes, mini-bosses and the warlord.

Duel

A rival wizard who learns how you dodge, in a small dungeon of its own.

How each mode works

Battle

You and a rival wizard each lead an orc army across open country: grassland ringed by cliffs and broken by rock outcrops. The match is best of five battles, each 500 orcs a side. Your army is blue, the rival's red.

Armies are split into regiments by unit type, each with its own banner: infantry (grunts), slingers, knights, shock troops (berserkers and brutes) and mystics (shamans and warlocks), with big regiments split in two. Your army deploys along the bottom of the field facing the rival's at the top, in lines (infantry and shock troops in front with knights on the flanks, slingers behind, mystics at the back), and you get fifteen seconds to rearrange them before the horns sound (Space attacks at once).

Tab switches between commander mode and spell mode. Battles open in commander mode: your wizard stands still, WASD pans the camera and the wheel zooms; left-click an orc to select its regiment (shift-click adds, drag a box to select several), right-click the ground to march the selection there and hold it, or right-click an enemy regiment (or the rival wizard) to send the selection against it. In spell mode you are the wizard again: WASD moves, the mouse casts. In either mode the regiment cards at the bottom select (shift-click adds), backquote selects all, and J, K and L order the selection to charge, march to the cursor and hold, or follow you.

Holding regiments fight anything that comes into reach; charging ones seek the nearest enemy. Brawlers close in, slingers and warlocks keep their range and shoot, knights charge, shamans heal the wounded.

Your spells change the fight: a fireball or blizzard wrecks a packed regiment, chain lightning jumps through the ranks, fear scatters them. The rival commands its own regiments (its line charges, its knights swing round a flank, its mystics guard it), turns its spells on your army and will come for you.

A battle is lost when your wizard falls or your whole army is destroyed. Spells build up as the match goes on: both wizards start with magic missile and one random spell; after every battle you choose one of three new spells while the rival learns one at random. Spells follow the duel rules, including the 3 second global cooldown after every spell except magic missile and lifesteal.

Survival

Descend ten floors of an orc-held dungeon, from The Upper Halls to the Throne of the Warlord. Each floor is a freshly generated maze of themed rooms (great hall, barracks, library, alchemy lab, crypt, throne room) packed with orc packs that guard their rooms until they notice you, then rally and hunt you down. The stairs in the throne room unseal once 40% of the floor is slain.

You start with only magic missile and learn the rest from scrolls: the 2D game's fifteen spells plus ten D&D spells, with tier 2 scrolls upgrading some of them. Potions heal or raise your maximum manergy, rings add passive effects, and everything carries down the stairs. Potions are scarce. Champion orcs and orc clerics and mages grow more common the deeper you go, and every floor hides a dark wizard who knows more random spells the deeper it lives. The warlord waits on the tenth floor. No school lockouts here.

Duel

One against one with a rival wizard in a small six-room dungeon, starting in the two rooms farthest apart. Walls block sight and bolts, so you can hide, flank and ambush; the rival reads your bolts in flight, dodges, shields, learns how you move and hunts you through the corridors.

Progressive duels (the default) start both wizards with magic missile and one random spell; scrolls and potions keep appearing around the map, and whoever reaches a scroll first learns a spell they lack. Classic duels give both every spell from the start. Every spell belongs to a school of magic (the tag beside it in the spell list): a cast locks the rest of its school for a few seconds, so weave spells from different schools. Magic missile and lifesteal never lock. Normal, hard and insane differ in how fast the rival reacts, how well it aims and which tricks it knows.
